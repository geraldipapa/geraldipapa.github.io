Nearly half the world's forcibly displaced are children, yet the dominant policy tool — unconditional cash — is justified on adult outcomes; its effects on early childhood development are assumed, not measured. This project adds a dedicated early-childhood-development arm to the ongoing randomized trial of cash and business training among roughly 4,000 refugee and host-community households in urban Nairobi (<a href="/publication/saturating-safety-net">Saturating the Safety Net</a>). Children aged 0&ndash;8 are assessed with validated instruments (CREDI, IDELA) alongside caregiver mental health, parenting stress, food security, and the home stimulation environment. Identification comes from experimental contrasts the original randomization preserves — training-plus-cash versus cash alone, and randomized differences in the timing of cash receipt — with the neighborhood-saturation design yielding community-level spillover estimates. Study design phase.
